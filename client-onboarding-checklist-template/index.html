1. Before kickoff

From the client's yes to the kickoff call

  1. Send the agreement or a written scope summary: what is included and what is not
  2. Agree the price, the payment schedule and what happens if a payment is late
  3. Send the deposit or first invoice, and record it in your invoice log
  4. Get the billing contact and any PO number or invoice rules they need
  5. Note the main contact, a backup contact, their time zone and preferred channel
  6. Send a welcome email with the kickoff date and the first things you need from them
  7. Send a short intake questionnaire: goals, audience, deadlines, examples they like
  8. Set up the client folder and project record in your own tools

2. Kickoff call

A 30 to 60 minute call that turns the scope into a plan

  1. Share a short agenda at least one working day before the call
  2. Hear the goal in the client's own words, and how they will judge success
  3. Name who gives feedback and who gives final approval
  4. Agree the first milestone and its date
  5. Agree how often you send updates and how fast they send feedback
  6. Walk through the scope once more, including how change requests are handled
  7. List risks, and anything you are waiting on from them
  8. Send a written recap of decisions and next steps the same day

3. First week

Access, inputs and the first update

  1. Get access to every account, file and tool you need, and test each login
  2. Check the files you received are usable: formats, sizes, latest versions
  3. Break the first milestone into tasks with dates
  4. Send the first progress update on the day you promised
  5. Follow up on anything still missing, and say how it affects the timeline

4. First 30 days

Deliver, check in, keep scope honest

  1. Deliver the first milestone, or a draft of it for review
  2. Ask the client how onboarding went and what would make working together easier
  3. Compare the work so far with the agreed scope, and raise any drift early
  4. Confirm the next invoice date and send it on schedule
  5. Book the next check-in or milestone review

Day numbers in the spreadsheet are a starting point (five days before kickoff to day 30); change them to fit the project. A planning checklist, not legal advice and not a contract. FAQ

Questions, answered straight

What are the steps to onboard a new client?

Four phases. Before kickoff: scope and price in writing, the deposit or first invoice, contacts and an intake questionnaire. The kickoff call: the goal in the client's words, who approves, the first milestone and the update rhythm, then a written recap. The first week: access, usable files and the first progress update. The first 30 days: the first milestone, a check-in on how onboarding went and a scope check. The free checklist on this page lists all 26 steps.

Is the checklist really free?

Yes. The .xlsx downloads straight from this page with no email or signup, and you can copy, change and reuse it for every client. The $12 Etsy kit is optional; it adds written emails and a printable PDF and Word file.

Does the free checklist open in Google Sheets?

It's a standard Excel file (.xlsx). Open it in Excel, or upload it to Google Drive and open it with Google Sheets. The status dropdown, the progress count and the due dates are ordinary spreadsheet formulas and validation.

What should a client onboarding checklist include?

At minimum: scope and price in writing, the deposit or first invoice, contacts and the channel you'll use, access to their files and accounts, a kickoff call that sets the first milestone and who approves it, and a first progress note in week one. The free checklist has 26 steps in four phases; the $12 kit's checklist has 26 items in five sections, A to E.

When do I send each kickoff email?

As the $12 kit notes on each one: the scope confirmation after the discovery call and before any work starts, the welcome email right after they say yes, and the kickoff recap within a few hours of the kickoff call.

Is this a contract?

No. The free checklist and the kit are checklists (the kit adds emails), not a legal contract. Both start with scope confirmed in writing; use your own agreement for the legal terms.

Do I need a CRM to use it?

No. The free spreadsheet works on its own. A few items in the kit say “CRM” and “invoice log”; a spreadsheet, a notes file or whatever client tracker you already use works the same way.

Are there other free client onboarding checklists?

Yes, and some are good; see the comparison on this page. Freshlance's free checklist is longer than our kit's (twelve sections), and OnboardHive has ten free onboarding emails. The $12 kit is for people who want one short printable checklist plus the three emails in one Word file.
