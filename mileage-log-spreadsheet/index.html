The record

What the IRS asks a mileage log to show

IRS Publication 463 (Table 5-1) lists what to record for car expenses. Here's each item and where it goes in this file. Our summary, not tax advice.

Publication 463 asks forWhere it goes in the file
The date of each business useTrip Log: Date
Your business destinationTrip Log: From and To
The business purposeTrip Log: Business purpose (a row without one is flagged)
The mileage for each business useTrip Log: one-way miles × round trip, or odometer start and end
Total miles for the yearVehicles: odometer on Jan 1 and Dec 31
The date you started using the car for businessVehicles: date placed in service
The cost of the car and any improvementsNot in this file. Keep the purchase paperwork; it matters most if you use actual costs instead of the standard rate.

Timing matters too: Publication 463 says to record trips at or near the time, and that a log kept weekly counts as a timely record. Source: irs.gov/publications/p463, chapter 5.

FAQ

Questions, answered straight

Is it a subscription?

No. It's a one-time download from Etsy. The apps compared above charge monthly or cap free use; this file is yours to keep.

Does it work in Google Sheets?

Yes. It's a real .xlsx file with live formulas and no macros. Open it in Excel, or in Google Sheets use File → Import → Upload.

Can I use it on my phone?

You can log trips in the Google Sheets app once the file is imported. Setting it up is easiest on a computer. It doesn't track GPS; you type each trip.

Do I need GPS tracking for my mileage log?

Publication 463 doesn't ask for GPS. It asks for a record made at or near the time of the trip, and says a log kept weekly counts as timely. Ask your preparer if you're unsure about your own situation.

Is the 2027 IRS rate filled in?

Not yet. The IRS hadn't published it on October 28, 2026; it announced the 2026 rate on December 29, 2025. The file is prefilled with $0.76 (the July–December 2026 rate), and a check on Start Here reminds you to type the 2027 rate into one cell.

Are commuting miles included?

Driving between home and a regular workplace is generally commuting, not business mileage (Publication 463). The Vehicles tab has a separate commuting estimate for the Schedule C vehicle questions. If you're unsure about a trip, ask your preparer.

Is this tax advice?

No. It's a record-keeping tool. It doesn't decide whether a trip is deductible or file anything. Orvian Market isn't affiliated with the IRS.
