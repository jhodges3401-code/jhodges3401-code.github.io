FAQ

Questions, answered straight

How do you politely remind a client about an overdue invoice?

Keep it short and factual: the invoice number, the amount, the original due date and the payment link, in the same email thread. The first reminder assumes it slipped through ("Friendly nudge"); the second asks whether it's queued with accounts payable or whether anything on the invoice needs correcting. No threats.

When should I send a payment reminder?

The schedule in the file: send the invoice on day 0, an optional heads-up 3 days before the due date for large invoices or new clients, a friendly reminder 1 day after the due date, a second follow-up about 7 days after, and a final note about 14 days after, only if your agreement lets you pause work.

What does the invoice tracker spreadsheet calculate?

Per invoice: the balance (amount minus amount paid), a status of Paid, Partial, Open or Overdue, and days overdue counted from the due date to today. The Summary tab totals invoiced, collected and outstanding, counts overdue invoices and shows the oldest one in days.

Is an invoice overdue on its due date?

No. The status only turns Overdue the day after the due date (the formula checks today is later than the due date). A partly paid invoice that passes its due date shows Overdue, not Partial, so it doesn't get forgotten.

What if a client pays too much?

The balance goes negative and the invoice reads Paid. The Summary's outstanding total adds up every balance, so an overpayment lowers it; note it and refund or credit the client so your totals stay right.

Can I charge a late fee with these templates?

The emails don't add late fees. Only charge one if your contract or invoice terms say so and it's allowed where you and your client are. This is writing and tracking help, not legal advice.

Does it work in Google Sheets?

Yes. The tracker is a real .xlsx with no macros; in Google Sheets use File → Import → Upload. The emails come as an editable Word file and a PDF.
