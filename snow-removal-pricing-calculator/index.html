The math, in plain words

Snow removal pricing formula

Work out what one push really costs you, then divide by what's left of the price after your card fee and the profit you want to keep.

Price floor = (cost per push + card fixed fee) ÷ (1 − card fee % − target margin)
Suggested price = the floor rounded up to your price step, never below your minimum charge

Cost per push, line by line

  1. Clearing time = area ÷ your clearing rate (sq ft per hour) × the depth multiplier. Walks get the same treatment with their own clearing rate.
  2. Job hours = clearing time + setup + travel to the site.
  3. Labor = job hours × what you pay per hour (you and any helpers).
  4. Equipment = clearing hours × each machine's cost per hour, not counting fuel.
  5. Fuel and de-icer for that visit (de-icer = pounds spread × cost per pound).
  6. Overhead per push: insurance, phone, software and the rest, spread over your visits.

Without a card fee the formula is just cost ÷ (1 − target margin). With no margin at all, it gives the break-even price.

Worked example (made-up numbers)

The same driveway as the example file below: 2,400 sq ft plus 300 sq ft of walks, 4 inches of snow, 35% target margin, card at 2.9% + $0.30.

  1. Clearing: 2,400 ÷ 18,000 × 1.35 = 0.18 h with the plow; 300 ÷ 2,500 × 1.35 = 0.162 h on the walks with a blower.
  2. Job hours: 0.18 + 0.162 + 10 min setup + 8 min travel = 0.64 h.
  3. Cost: equipment $9.07 + fuel $1.79 + de-icer $9.50 + labor $19.26 + overhead $6.00 = $45.62.
  4. Floor: ($45.62 + $0.30) ÷ (1 − 0.029 − 0.35) = $45.92 ÷ 0.621 = $73.95.
  5. Suggested: rounded up to the next $5 = $75.00 (above the $45 minimum).
  6. Check: card fee $2.48, profit $75.00 − $45.62 − $2.48 = $26.90, 35.9% of the price.

Per inch, per hour, per sq ft and seasonal

By snow depth (per inch)

Deeper snow mostly costs you time, so depth works as a multiplier on clearing time rather than a flat amount per inch. The example tiers are under 2 in ×1.0, 2 to under 4 in ×1.15, 4 to under 6 in ×1.35, 6 to under 9 in ×1.7, 9 to under 12 in ×2.1 and 12 in or more ×2.8. These are made-up starting points; time a few of your own pushes and replace them. Run the formula once per tier and you have a price list by depth (the calculator below does that).

Per hour and per sq ft

These are the per-push price seen another way, not separate methods. In the example, $75.00 ÷ 0.642 job hours is about $116.82 per job hour, and $75.00 ÷ 2,700 sq ft is $27.78 per 1,000 sq ft. They only carry over to sites with similar size, layout, depth and drive time, so work each site out from its own costs.

Seasonal contract

Contract floor = (normal-season events × cost per push + season costs for the client + card fixed fee × number of payments) ÷ (1 − card fee % − target margin). For the example, 12 events, $25 of season costs and 4 card payments give $923.79, rounded up to $925. Then check a heavy winter with the contract check.

What the formula leaves to you

Your clearing rates, depth multipliers, pay and overhead are your numbers, and they decide the price. Nothing here is a typical or market rate; compare the result with what you know of local prices before you quote.

How the price is worked out

Questions

What is the snow removal pricing formula?

Price floor = (your cost per push + any card fixed fee) ÷ (1 − card fee % − target margin). Cost per push is labor, equipment, fuel, de-icer and overhead for one visit, with clearing time set by the area, your clearing rate and a snow depth multiplier. Round the floor up to a price you would quote, and never go below your minimum charge. The worked example on this page goes from $45.62 of cost to a $75.00 price.

Use the same formula with the lot's numbers: plowable area and the clearing rate of the machine you use there, everyone's pay while on site, de-icer for the area you treat, and travel.
