FAQ

Questions, answered straight

How do you budget when your income changes every month?

Plan from money that has actually arrived, not from invoices you expect. Cover your essentials and your non-monthly bills first, hold back taxes and a small cash buffer, and treat whatever is left as the only money for flexible spending. This workbook does that subtraction for any month you pick.

Does it calculate my taxes?

No. You choose the percentage to hold back (the example file uses 25% as a placeholder) and it applies that to income you mark as taxable. What you actually owe depends on your whole tax picture, so set the rate with your tax plan or a professional. It's a planning tool, not tax advice.

Why does it only count money I've received?

Because an unpaid invoice can't pay rent. The Monthly Income tab has an Expected column and an Actual Received column; Safe-to-Spend reads only Actual Received. In the example, a $900 February invoice that hasn't been paid shows as expected but adds nothing to February's safe-to-spend.

What happens in a month when income doesn't cover the basics?

Safe-to-spend shows $0 instead of a negative number, which is the signal to pause flexible spending and draw on savings. The example's February does exactly that.

Is it a subscription?

No. It's a one-time download from Etsy. There's no account and no bank connection; you type the numbers in.

Does it work in Google Sheets?

Yes. It's a real .xlsx file with live formulas and no macros. Open it in Excel, or in Google Sheets use File → Import → Upload.

Is it only for freelancers?

No. It suits anyone paid unevenly: freelancers, contractors, gig workers, commission sales and seasonal work. Mark income you don't owe tax on as not taxable and it's left out of the tax hold-back.
