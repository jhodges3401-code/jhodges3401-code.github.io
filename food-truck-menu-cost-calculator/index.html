The math, in plain words

Food truck menu pricing formula

Divide what one plate costs you in food by the share of the price you want that food to be. The target is a number you choose.

Suggested menu price = plate food cost ÷ target food cost %
Then round that price up to your price step.

  1. Plate food cost is the ingredients in one serving, after trim and cooking loss.
  2. Target food cost % is the share of the menu price you want those ingredients to be. You set it. A lower target means a higher price.
  3. Food cost % at the price you charge = plate food cost ÷ that price. Packaging, labor and card fees stay out of it.
  4. Gross profit per plate = menu price − plate food cost − packaging.

A rule of thumb is only a starting point you pick for your own truck, then check against prices you already sell. This page does not supply a typical percent. The made-up taco below uses 30% so the figures match: $2.31 ÷ 0.30 = $7.70, which rounds up to $8.00 at a $0.50 step. At $8.00, food cost is 28.9% and gross profit after $0.35 packaging is $5.34.

Event break-even, in the same words

One order keeps the average ticket minus food, packaging, the card fee and any commission. Break-even orders = (staff pay + event fee + other costs) ÷ that amount, rounded up. The made-up brewery night below keeps about $4.89 on an order against $444 of those costs, so it breaks even at 91 orders and, at 100 orders, keeps $45.15. You type how many orders you expect. The math does not forecast sales.

Questions

How do I price a food truck menu?

Add up the ingredient cost of one plate after yield. Suggested menu price = that plate food cost ÷ your target food cost %. Round the result up to your price step. In the made-up taco example, $2.31 ÷ 30% = $7.70, which rounds up to $8.00 at a $0.50 step. At $8.00 the food cost is 28.9% and gross profit after $0.35 packaging is $5.34. The free calculator does this for one plate from numbers you type. It is an estimate from those numbers, not a forecast of what people will pay.

What food cost percent should I target?

The target is yours: the share of the menu price you want the food itself to be. A lower target produces a higher price. A rule of thumb is only a starting point you pick for your own truck, then check against prices you already sell. This page does not supply a typical percent, and it has no average menu prices. The made-up example uses 30% so the taco figures match. On that menu the chicken plate at $8.00 sits inside a 28% to 32% band, and two other plates are flagged above it.

How do I account for packaging and card fees?

Leave both out of food cost %. Food cost % is plate food cost ÷ menu price. Packaging comes off after that: gross profit per plate = price − food cost − packaging. Card fees, staff pay, commission and the booth fee are event costs. In the made-up brewery night, packaging is $0.462 an order ($46.20 on 100 orders) and the card fee is 49.14 ÷ 959 of sales ($49.14). The free event calculator subtracts both. The spreadsheet keeps packaging out of food cost % and counts card fees in the Event Planner.

How many orders does it take to break even at an event?

Take what is left of the average ticket after food, packaging, the card fee and any commission. Add staff pay, the event fee and other costs. Break-even orders = those fixed costs ÷ the amount left on one order, rounded up. In the made-up brewery night about $4.89 is left on each order and the fixed costs are $444, so break-even is 91 orders. The example sells 100 and keeps $45.15, which is 4.7% of the $959.00. You type the order count. Nothing here forecasts how many people will show up.
