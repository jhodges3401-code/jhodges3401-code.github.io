FAQ

Questions, answered straight

How do I tell a client I'm raising my rates?

In a short one-to-one email: the new rate as a number, the date it starts, and what happens to work already booked (usually it stays at the current rate). Email 2 in the file does exactly that and offers to hold a block of hours at the current rate before the change.

How much notice should I give before a rate increase?

The file says at least 30 days for existing clients. If your contract sets a notice period, that one wins. For a January 1 increase, 30 days means sending by December 2.

How much should I raise my rates?

The guide in the file notes many freelancers move in steps, for example 10–20%, rather than one big jump, and says to pick what your numbers support. On $85 an hour, 10% is $93.50, 15% is $97.75 and 20% is $102.

What do I say if a client pushes back on the new rate?

Email 4: thank them, keep the new rate, and give three options instead of a discount: book a block of hours at the current rate before the date, trim the scope so the monthly total stays close to today's, or move to a fixed project or smaller retainer. End with a question.

Should I raise rates for new clients first?

Yes, if you're underpriced. The guide calls fixing your new-client rate the change you control fastest: quote it from the first proposal (Email 1) and don't carry old pricing into new work.

Is there a free rate increase letter template?

Yes, several good ones; see the comparison on this page. SayAnchor's guide has eight free templates, including a reply to pushback. This kit is for people who want four tidy, editable emails plus a checklist in one Word file.

What format are the templates?
