FAQ

Questions, answered straight

How do I calculate my freelance hourly rate?

Work backward from the take-home pay you want. Gross it up for tax and a profit buffer, add your yearly business costs, then divide by the hours you can actually bill, not the hours you work. With the Designer preset that's $65,000 take-home, 28% tax and a 15% buffer, plus $8,300 of costs, over 960 billable hours: $119.28 an hour.

What's the difference between the minimum and the recommended rate?

The minimum (the sheet calls it the survival rate) is the hourly rate that just covers your take-home goal, tax estimate, buffer and overhead at your billable hours. The recommended rate adds 20% on top for safety, and the day, week and retainer figures are built from the recommended rate.

What billable utilization should I use?

Be honest. Utilization is the share of your working hours a client actually pays for; sales, admin, revisions and unpaid discovery all come out of it. The presets use 45% to 60%, and the what-if table shows your yearly revenue at 40% to 75% so you can see how much it matters.

Does it tell me what other freelancers charge?

No. It does the math from your own goal, costs and hours. The Market Check tab is there for you to record rates you've actually seen in your field; its example ranges are placeholders, not market data.

Does it work in Google Sheets?

Yes. It's a real .xlsx file with live formulas and no macros. Open it in Excel, or in Google Sheets use File → Import → Upload.

Is the tax rate in the sheet accurate for me?

It's an estimate you type in. The presets start at 28% or 30% as placeholders. Your real effective rate depends on your whole tax picture, so set it with your tax plan or a professional. It's a planning tool, not tax advice.
